# 04. Shot Ke Peeche (either topic, robotics first)

**Logline:** A scrappy phone clip of students and crew doing something that looks ridiculous, then a hard cut to the finished cinematic shot it produced.

**Length:** 20s. **Format:** 9:16. **Language:** Hinglish, on-screen text only.

**Reference:** [phone behind-the-scenes, then the graded shot](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DajqRCJJXOM/) (18.2M on a 4.5K-follower college batch account, 4,043x). See [00-references.md](00-references.md).

**Why it should travel:** it is the highest multiple of any reference in this research, and it turns the crew into content. The phone half is the hook; the cinematic half is the payoff. It also costs nothing extra, because it is made from footage of the other four shoots.

## Structure

| Part | Time | What the viewer sees |
|---|---|---|
| The mess | 0-9s | Vertical phone footage, ungraded, shaky, real sound. Someone lying on the floor with a camera. A student holding a torch at an odd angle. Another fanning a notebook to move dust or mist. A teacher looking on, baffled. |
| The cut | 9-10s | One beat of black. Sound drops out. |
| The shot | 10-18s | The finished frame: letterboxed, graded, slow motion, shallow focus. Exactly what that chaos was making. |
| The tag | 18-20s | Back to the phone: everyone crowding the monitor, reacting. |

## Three versions to cut from the other shoots

| Version | The mess (phone) | The shot (cinema camera) |
|---|---|---|
| A. Robot hero shot | Camera operator flat on the lab floor; two students crouched out of frame holding a phone torch and a sheet of white paper as a reflector; a third nudging the robot with a ruler | From [01](01-pehli-baar-chala.md): the low-angle slow-motion shot of the robot rolling toward the lens, LEDs glowing |
| B. Boots | Operator lying in the dust at dawn; the squad marching straight at them; someone pulling the operator back by the ankles at the last second | From [02](02-paanch-baje.md): boots hitting the ground in step, front boot sharp |

Start with version A.

## How to shoot the phone half

- A student or a teacher films it, not the crew. Vertical, handheld, no stabiliser.
- Film wide enough to see the whole odd setup in one frame.
- Keep the real sound: instructions being called, laughter, "ek baar aur".
- Do not light it and do not grade it. The contrast with the second half is the point.

## Look of the cinematic half

- Add black bars top and bottom so it reads as "film" the instant it appears.
- Slow motion at 50 or 100 fps.
- Full grade, score comes in on the cut.

## On-screen text

- 0s, over the phone clip: `Yeh log kar kya rahe hain? 😂`
- 10s, over the shot: nothing for two seconds, then `Yeh.`
- 18s: `Shot: Class {{class}} ke saath.`

## Edit notes

- The phone half must be funny or strange with sound off in the first second. Pick the oddest-looking moment for frame one.
- One hard cut. No transition effect.
- End on a real reaction at the monitor.

## Versions

- **Organic, 20s:** as above. This one is organic only; it is not an ad.
- **Series:** post A, B and C a week apart. Each one also sends viewers to the full film it came from.

## Needs from the school

1. Someone assigned to film the crew on a phone during every shoot. This has to be decided before the shoots, not after.
2. Consent covering the behind-the-scenes footage as well as the films.
3. The class of the students who helped, for the last card.

## Risk

If the behind-the-scenes clip is tidy, there is no hook. It needs to look like real confusion.
