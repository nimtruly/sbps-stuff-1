# 03. Ek Hi Bachcha (robotics + NDA)

**Logline:** A drill film and a robotics film cut together shot for shot, until the last frame shows they are the same student.

**Length:** 25s. **Format:** 9:16. **Language:** no speech until one line at the end.

**References:** ["first day / last day of school"](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DW8H7vtkXA1/) (22.1M, 3,484x followers) for match cuts; [school nostalgia macro film](https://www.instagram.com/reel/C_hxpnny8YZ/) (49.5M) for the close-up language. See [00-references.md](00-references.md).

**Why it should travel:** match cuts are satisfying to watch and people replay them to catch each pair. It is also the only one of the five that no other school can copy, because it needs both programmes in one child.

## The idea in one picture

Every drill shot has a twin in the robotics lab with the same framing, the same movement and the same shape in the frame. The edit cuts between the twins on the action.

## Look

- **Two palettes.** Drill: cold, early morning, blue-grey. Lab: warm, tungsten, amber. The colour change is how the viewer knows which world they are in.
- **Lenses:** the same lens and the same distance for both halves of each pair. Mark it on the shot sheet.
- **Aperture:** wide open throughout.
- **Frame rate:** 50 fps for everything so any pair can be slowed to match.
- **Movement:** identical in both halves. If the drill shot pushes in, so does the lab shot.
- **Framing discipline:** shoot the drill half first, take a still from the monitor, and line up the lab half against it.

## The pairs

| # | Time | Drill (cold) | Robotics (warm) | Cut on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0-3s | Macro: hand pulls a boot lace tight | Macro: the same hand pulls a cable tie tight on a robot chassis | The pull |
| 2 | 3-6s | Low angle: a boot stamps down at "Savdhan" | Low angle: a robot wheel drops onto the table | The impact |
| 3 | 6-9s | Top-down: boots lined up toe to toe | Top-down: components lined up in a row | The static frame, a hard cut on the beat |
| 4 | 9-12s | Profile, 85mm: eyes front, unblinking | Profile, 85mm: eyes on a circuit, unblinking | A slow push-in that continues across the cut |
| 5 | 12-15s | Squad turns right together | A servo arm turns right | The turn |
| 6 | 15-18s | Hand rises in a salute | Hand rises to lower safety glasses | The rise |
| 7 | 18-21s | Wide from behind: the squad marching away into mist | Wide from behind: the robot rolling away down a corridor | Direction of travel |
| 8 | 21-25s | Split frame, top and bottom: left half of the face in cold light, right half in warm light. It resolves into one portrait, the student looking into the lens | | A slow dissolve |

**Sound:** each cut lands on a sound from the scene: lace, stamp, click, breath, turn, cloth, motor. A low pulse underneath that builds. No melody until pair 7.

**The one line**, student's voice, over shot 8: `"Dono main hi hoon."`

**On-screen text:** at 22s, `Subah drill. Dopahar robotics.` Use the real timetable; if robotics is not in the afternoon, change the line to match. Logo in the last second.

## Edit notes

- Three seconds per pair, about 1.5 seconds each side. Tight.
- The first pair has to read with sound off: same hand, same action, different colour.
- Do not show the face until pair 4. Do not show the whole face until shot 8.

## Versions

- **Organic, 25s:** as above.
- **Ad cut, 15s:** pairs 1, 2, 5, 6, then shot 8 with an admissions end card.
- **Stills:** the eight paired frames make a carousel post for free.

## Needs from the school

1. One student who is genuinely in both the NDA programme and robotics. If there is no such student, do not fake it: use two students and end on them side by side with the line "Ek hi school."
2. The real timetable for the text card.
3. A robot with a wheel, a servo arm, and visible wiring.
4. Consent for the lead and for the squad.
5. Two half-days: a dawn session on the ground, then the lab with the room darkened.

## Risk

If the framing of a pair is off by even a little, the match cut fails. This one needs a shot sheet with monitor stills, and it is worth a day of the crew's time before the shoot.
