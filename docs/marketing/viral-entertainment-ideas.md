# SBPS reels: 10 ideas built on viral entertainment formats

Research date: 16 Nov 2026. For [@sbpsdehradun](https://www.instagram.com/sbpsdehradun/).
Goal: virality first. Cinematic, funny, promotes robotics in schools and SBPS with it.

This file is new. Earlier work is unchanged:
- Phone-shot scripts: `docs/marketing/reels-robotics-nda-outlier-scripts.md`
- Cinematic concepts: `docs/marketing/cinematic/`

## 1. What I looked at

- Tanmay Bhat's reels tab (24 most recent reels with view counts) and 11 reels by Vir Saini / Pink Sauce Studios.
- 20 more Instagram searches: funny Indian ads, brand films, comedy sketches, Bollywood spoofs, movie recreations, heist parodies, robot comedy, teacher-student comedy, desi parents.
- About 750 reels catalogued, view counts pulled for about 370.
- 30 downloaded. I looked at frame sheets for 23 of them, and at denser frames for Vir Saini's biggest reel.

**Limits, stated up front:**
- Dialogue transcription mostly failed on Hinglish comedy (local Whisper, small model). I got usable dialogue for two Tanmay Bhat reels. For the rest I read the burned-in subtitles and captions, so I know each premise and structure but not every line.
- Frame sheets do not show timing or cut rhythm. Watch the links for that.
- Instagram search is personalised. This is a strong sample, not a census.
- Nobody can promise virality. These ideas copy the mechanism of reels that earned it; they raise the odds, nothing more.

## 3. The nine mechanisms that earned the views

| # | Mechanism | Best evidence | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | **Swap two things and play it straight** | Vir Saini's "If baniyans..." film: men's vests treated the way bras are, shot like a feature. 23.4M | One sentence of premise, then every scene is a new joke from the same swap. The caption is a question, so people answer. |
| B | **Say the quiet part out loud** | Tanmay Bhat x Noice: Diwali guests say what they actually think ("this is my friend you'll never meet again"). 26.9M. Also "Desi moms give no privacy", 106M | Recognition. People tag whoever it describes. |
| C | **Serious format, silly subject** | "Save Bosses": a charity appeal for overworked CEOs. 10.8M. A satire of hustle culture, 12.6M. Vir Saini's "An outsider's guide to..." with exhibits and chapter cards | The more solemn the delivery, the funnier it gets. Needs a real cinematic look, which suits your crew. |
| D | **The child outsmarts the adult** | Virgin Mobile ad, daughter vs parents, re-posted with the scoreline "Indian parents: 0, Daughter: 1". 38.2M. Classroom "6+9=15" sketch, 50.4M. Teacher-student sketch, 121M | Everyone roots for the kid. A scoreline makes it shareable. |
| E | **Calm people, absurd event, product as the punchline** | Noice "broken window", 16.1M. Shera ceiling geckos, 18.9M. CRED monster on a date, 56M | Beautiful, quiet, deadpan photography, then something ridiculous. The brand appears only in the last three seconds. |
| F | **Blockbuster treatment of a small thing** | "The Potato Heist", 57.9M. "Bollywood actors as press-wale bhaiya", 31.4M. Karan Johar spoof, 110M | Genre is instantly readable. The gap between the stakes and the style is the joke. |
| G | **A robot with a human flaw** | Robot comedy reels run from 24M to 134M: a robot that bullies, sulks, or gets it wrong. "AI will replace humans / le humans", 69.8M | People enjoy a machine being petty or useless. A robot that works perfectly is not funny. |
| H | **One visual gag that loops** | Barbershop gag, 9 seconds, 604M. Cup-on-the-pavement gag, 12 seconds, 504M | Short enough to replay three times before you scroll. The biggest numbers in the whole sample. |
| I | **Big budget next to no budget** | A Hollywood scene re-created with household items, shown side by side. 68M | The comparison is the content. |

Two things the whole sample agrees on:
1. **The brand shows up at the end**, in three seconds or less.
2. **The premise fits in one line.** If the idea needs explaining, it is two ideas.

## 4. The ten ideas

Each one names the mechanism it uses. Lines are in Hinglish and are a guide; actors should say them their own way. `{{braces}}` are for the school to fill in. These are fiction, so the stories can be invented, but nothing claims a real achievement of the school. The only programme claim used anywhere is that SBPS teaches robotics.

Rules for all ten:
- No logo or school name before the last three seconds.
- Cinema camera, shallow focus, proper grade, clean recorded sound. The comedy comes from playing it straight, so it must look like a real film.
- Burned-in subtitles throughout.
- The caption is a question.

### 1. Agar Robotics Cricket Hota (mechanism A)

**One line:** A world where India treats school robotics exactly the way it treats cricket.

**Length:** 60-75s. **Reference:** [Vir Saini, "If baniyans..."](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DZM9Z6NM0XA/) (23.4M).

| Scene | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0-4s | Title card over a slow-motion shot of a child tightening a screw, crowd roar underneath: `Agar robotics... cricket hota.` |
| Chai stall | Two uncles with a newspaper. "Sharma ji ke bete ka sensor form mein nahi hai." "Selection toh pakka Gupta ka hi hoga, uske papa ki pehchaan hai." |
| Gully | Kids in the lane with a cardboard robot. A ball of wire goes through a window. An aunty at the door: "Yahan robot mat banao! Park mein jao!" |
| Living room | A whole family frozen in front of the TV. On screen, a child is debugging. Dad whispers: "Bas ek semicolon. Bas ek." Mum has stopped mid-roti. |
| Commentary box | Two commentators, headsets on. "Aur yeh dekhiye, kya khoobsurat soldering. Classical." "Bilkul, technique mein koi kami nahi." |
| Wedding | A relative points at a small boy. "Yeh? Under-12 state robotics khelta hai." Everyone turns. The rishta aunty takes out a notebook. |
| Street | A robot rolls past. Traffic stops. People film it. A hawker sells jerseys with a kid's name on them. |
| Final | The real robotics room at SBPS. A child looks up from a kit, straight at the lens. Silence. Card: `Kuch schools mein yeh duniya already hai.` Logo. |

**Cinematic notes:** each scene has its own palette and one hero shot (the frozen family in a single wide; the commentary box in tight profile with a rack focus between the two men). Stadium sound mixed under ordinary rooms.

**Caption:** `Aap is duniya mein rehna chahoge?`

**Needs:** 6 to 8 adult actors (staff and parents work well), three locations beyond the school.

### 2. Robot Bachao (mechanism C)

**One line:** A solemn charity appeal on behalf of robotics kits locked in school cupboards.

**Length:** 45-60s. **Reference:** ["Save Bosses"](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DPEhNaLkrC_/) (10.8M).

| Time | Picture | Voice-over (grave, slow, male or female) |
|---|---|---|
| 0-5s | Black and white. A dusty cupboard. Through the gap in the door, one small LED blinking. | "Is waqt, kahin na kahin, ek robot almari mein band hai." |
| 5-15s | A robot at a classroom window, watching children play outside. Rain on the glass. | "Usne kabhi dhoop nahi dekhi. Kabhi ek line follow nahi ki." |
| 15-25s | Close on a lock. A register that reads "Robotics kit: issued 0 times". A cobweb between two wheels. | "Use sirf inspection ke din bahar nikala jaata hai. Photo ke liye." |
| 25-35s | A child's hand reaches toward the cupboard. A teacher's hand gently closes it. | "Aap chahein toh yeh badal sakta hai." |
| 35-45s | Colour floods in. SBPS students pull a kit out, wheels spinning. The robot rolls into sunlight. | "Sirf ek period. Hafte mein ek baar. Ek robot ki zindagi badal sakti hai." |
| 45-55s | The robot on the playground with children running beside it. Freeze frame, like an NGO ad. | "Robot bachao. Robot chalao." |
| End | Card: `SBPS mein robot almari mein nahi rehte.` Logo. | |

**Cinematic notes:** copy the grammar of real charity appeals exactly: slow push-ins, piano, a helpline-style lower third that reads `Robot Helpline: apne school se poochiye`. No statistics anywhere, so nothing needs sourcing.

**Caption:** `Aapke school ka robot kahan hai?` This is the comment bait: people will name their schools.

**Risk:** it teases other schools. Keep it about cupboards, never about a named school.

### 3. Honest Parent-Teacher Meeting (mechanism B)

**One line:** A parent-teacher meeting where everyone says what they are really thinking.

**Length:** 45-60s. **Reference:** [Tanmay Bhat x Noice](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DPtTQzVDKJ1/) (26.9M).

Shot as a prestige drama: two-shots across a desk, shallow focus, soft window light, nobody smiling.

- **Parent 1:** "Ma'am, mera beta ghar pe kuch nahi karta. Main chahti hoon aap usse theek kar dein, taaki mujhe na karna pade."
- **Teacher, warmly:** "Bilkul. Aur main chahti hoon aap usse theek kar dein, taaki mujhe na karna pade."
- **Parent 2:** "Mujhe marks se farak nahi padta." Beat. "Bas Sharma ji ke bete se zyada hone chahiye."
- **Father, on his phone the whole time:** "Main yahan sirf isliye aaya hoon ki meri wife ne bola tha." He does not look up.
- **Child, to camera:** "Mujhe laga tha meri baat hogi."
- **Robotics teacher, last desk:** "Aapka beta poora din screen dekhta hai."
- **Parent, horrified:** "Wahi toh problem hai!"
- **Robotics teacher:** "Ab woh screen pe code likh raha hai. Kal usne ek robot chalaya."
- **Parent, long pause:** "...toh ab main kya complain karoon?"

Card: `Kuch complaints hum solve kar dete hain.` Logo.

**Caption:** `Kaun sa parent aapke ghar ka hai?`

**Needs:** four adults who can play it dead straight, one child. Real teachers playing themselves is funnier than actors.

### 4. Papa: 0, Class 7: 1 (mechanism D)

**One line:** An engineer father cannot get the robot to work. His daughter can, without looking up.

**Length:** 30-40s. **Reference:** [Virgin Mobile ad re-post](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DQdp5YwDDQ8/) (38.2M), ["6+9=15" classroom sketch](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DY_1-q-gyr1/) (50.4M).

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0-4s | Living room, evening. A father, sleeves rolled up, over a robot kit. "Beta, main engineer hoon. Hamare zamane mein toh..." |
| 4-14s | Montage of confidence: reading the manual upside down, blowing on a wire, tapping it like a TV remote. Nothing. He calls a friend: "Haan, woh... ek chhota sa doubt tha." |
| 14-20s | The daughter, eating, not looking up. "Papa." "Ek minute beta." "Papa." "Bola na ek minute." |
| 20-26s | She reaches over with one hand, flips a single switch. The robot starts and drives off the table into his lap. She keeps eating. |
| 26-32s | He stares at it. "...yeh toh main bhi kar raha tha." |
| End | Scoreline in the corner like a cricket graphic: `Papa: 0   Class {{class}}: 1`. Card: `School mein seekha.` Logo. |

**Cinematic notes:** warm tungsten, long lens on the father's face, the daughter always soft in the foreground until her one move, then rack focus to her.

**Caption:** `Tag karo us papa ko jo "hamare zamane mein" bolte hain.`

**Series:** Mummy: 0, Dadaji: 0, Tuition wale sir: 0.

### 5. Homework Heist (mechanism F)

**One line:** A full heist film in which a crew of students uses a robot to steal one samosa from the staff room.

**Length:** 60s. **Reference:** ["The Potato Heist"](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dc8u5q5qCH5/) (57.9M).

- **The plan (0-15s):** a darkened classroom, a blueprint of the staff room on the blackboard drawn in chalk. The leader, deadly serious: "Target: ek samosa. Staff room. Lunch break. Humare paas chaar minute hain." Team introductions with freeze-frames and name cards: `Dimaag`, `Driver`, `Lookout`, `Naya ladka`.
- **The walk (15-22s):** slow-motion corridor walk, four abreast, school bags like duffel bags.
- **The job (22-45s):** the robot is lowered to the floor and drives under the staff-room door. Robot's-eye view: chair legs, a teacher's sandals, a dropped pen. A close call when a teacher looks down; the robot freezes next to a dustbin. A plate is nudged. The samosa lands on the robot.
- **The escape (45-52s):** the robot reverses out. Fist bumps in silence.
- **The twist (52-60s):** the principal is standing behind them, holding a plate of samosas. "Robotics practical. Full marks." Beat. "Samosa wapas rakho."

Card: `Robotics. Seriously sikhaate hain.` Logo.

**Cinematic notes:** teal and orange grade, split screens, a ticking score, laser-style shafts of light from window blinds. The robot's-eye camera is a small action camera taped to the chassis.

**Caption:** `Aapki team mein aap kaun hote?`

**Needs:** a robot that drives under a door gap, the principal's cameo. The principal is what makes it shareable among parents.

### 6. Robot Ka Pehla Din (mechanism G, mockumentary)

**One line:** A documentary crew follows a new student on his first day. The new student is a robot, and he is not coping.

**Length:** 60s, built as a series. **Reference:** robot-with-a-flaw reels, 24M to 134M (for example ["AI will replace humans"](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DHRD_PfTpd9/), 69.8M).

Handheld, zooms mid-shot, talking-head interviews against a plain wall.

- **Assembly:** everyone in rows. The robot arrives late and stops in the wrong line. A prefect moves it. It rolls back.
- **Interview, class teacher:** "Bachcha achha hai. Bas sunta nahi hai. Literally. Mic nahi laga hai usme."
- **Classroom:** "Roll number 34?" Silence. A single beep. "Present bolo beta." Beep.
- **Interview, bench partner:** "Thoda shy hai. Aur tiffin share nahi karta. Charger share karta hai."
- **Punishment:** the robot outside the classroom door, facing the wall, next to a boy doing the same. They look at each other.
- **Lunch:** children eating. The robot plugged into a wall socket, alone. A girl slides her chair over and sits beside it.
- **Interview, the girl:** "Sab bolte hain usko kuch nahi aata. Pehle din kisko aata hai?"
- **Last shot:** end of day, the robot rolling out of the gate in a line with the others, a tiny school bag taped to it.

Card: `Naye admission. Sabke liye.` Logo.

**Caption:** `Iska naam kya rakhein?`

**Series:** exam day, sports day, the robot gets a complaint in its diary, the robot at NDA drill (see idea 9).

**Why this is the strongest long-term bet:** a recurring character gives people a reason to follow, which a one-off film cannot.

### 7. Hero Ki Entry (mechanism F, Bollywood)

**One line:** A full Bollywood hero entrance, for a very small robot.

**Length:** 20-25s. **Reference:** [Bollywood spoofs](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DfR1Q0NogHX/), 31M to 110M.

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0-3s | A school gate opens in slow motion. Dust. Wind. A crowd of students turns to look. A dhol starts. |
| 3-9s | Low angle, backlit: a silhouette at the gate. Children shield their eyes. Someone drops their books. A teacher takes off her glasses. |
| 9-14s | The reveal. It is a robot about the size of a lunchbox, wearing tiny sunglasses, rolling very slowly. The camera tracks it with full seriousness. Petals fall. |
| 14-19s | It reaches a small bump in the path. It stops. It tries again. It tips over. The dhol stops mid-beat. |
| 19-23s | Silence. A child walks over, sets it upright, pats it. It carries on. The dhol restarts, quieter. |

Card: `Hero abhi training mein hai.` Logo.

**Cinematic notes:** 100 fps for the crowd, a wind machine or a large fan, a fog of coloured powder, anamorphic-style flare from a backlight. Use an original dhol-and-brass track, not a film song, so the same cut can be boosted.

**Caption:** `Kis hero ki entry yaad aa gayi?`

### 8. Expectation vs Class 6 (mechanism I)

**One line:** A glossy "robot of the future" advert on top, a Class 6 cardboard robot underneath doing the same moves. Only one of them is real.

**Length:** 20-30s. **Reference:** [movie scene re-created three ways](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DOdd6rdDNhy/) (68M).

The frame is split, top and bottom, with labels.

- **Top, `Ad wala robot`:** the crew shoots a parody of a tech advert. A sleek prop on a turntable, lens flares, a deep voice: "The future. Is here." It is plainly a painted box.
- **Bottom, `Class 6 wala robot`:** cardboard, tape, a visible battery pack, a child's handwriting on the side.
- **Beat by beat:** the top robot "rotates" (the turntable spins). The bottom one actually turns. The top one "sees" (a red light). The bottom one stops before a wall. The top one "walks" (a hand slides it from out of frame, and the hand is visible). The bottom one drives.
- **The turn:** the turntable stops. A crew member lifts the top robot. It is hollow. The bottom robot rolls into the top frame and bumps it over.

Card: `Dikhne mein nahi. Chalne mein.` Logo.

**Caption:** `Kaun sa asli hai?`

**Note:** both halves are shot by your crew. Do not use footage from a real film or a real company's advert.

### 9. Ustaad vs Robot (mechanism E, robotics meets NDA)

**One line:** A drill instructor tries to command a robot. It does not listen. A small child fixes it with a laptop.

**Length:** 30-40s. **Reference:** deadpan ads such as [Noice "broken window"](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Dba2B7BMXwv/) (16.1M).

| Time | Beat |
|---|---|
| 0-5s | Dawn. A squad at attention. The instructor walks the line, inspecting. He stops at the end. He looks down. A robot is in the line. |
| 5-12s | "Savdhan!" The squad snaps. The robot rolls forward. "Savdhan!" It keeps going. He walks beside it, shouting commands into it at close range. It drives gently into his boot. |
| 12-18s | He tries everything: louder, softer, in English, a whistle. The squad is desperately not laughing. |
| 18-25s | A very small student steps out of line with a laptop. "Sir, yeh awaaz nahi samajhta. Isse likh ke bolna padta hai." Types four keys. The robot halts, perfectly. |
| 25-32s | The instructor looks at the robot. At the child. At the squad. "Kal se tum sab ko bhi likh ke bolunga." |

Card: `Discipline bhi. Robotics bhi.` Logo.

**Cinematic notes:** this is the one that shows both programmes. Shoot it at blue hour with a long lens, as seriously as the drill film in `cinematic/02-paanch-baje.md`, so the robot is the only thing out of place.

**Caption:** `Ustaad ko kaun samjhaye?`

**Needs:** the real NDA instructor, playing himself. If he will not, do not cast an actor as a serving soldier; use a PT teacher.

### 10. Chalk (mechanism H, the 10-second loop)

**One line:** A robot brings the teacher her chalk. Every time she reaches for it, it reverses.

**Length:** 9-12s. **Reference:** [barbershop gag](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DQk6deIkjKZ/) (604M, 9s), [cup gag](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DVBjHxEDUyA/) (504M, 12s).

One locked shot, side on, at desk height.

- A teacher at the blackboard holds out her hand without looking. A robot rolls up with a piece of chalk on top.
- She reaches. It reverses a few centimetres.
- She leans. It reverses again.
- She turns and looks at it. It rolls forward, innocent.
- She reaches. It reverses. The class loses it.
- Last frame: her hand out again, the robot rolling up, exactly as the first frame.

No card, no logo in the video. School name in the caption only. This one exists to be replayed and sent.

**Caption:** `Dubara dekho.`

**Needs:** a remote-controlled robot and a student driving it from under a desk. Shoot ten takes; real laughter cannot be scripted.

## 5. Which to make first

| Rank | Idea | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10. Chalk | Cheapest, shortest, and the loop-gag format has the biggest numbers in the entire sample. Shoot it in an hour. |
| 2 | 6. Robot Ka Pehla Din | A character people can follow. Gives the account a reason to be followed, not only watched. |
| 3 | 1. Agar Robotics Cricket Hota | The most ambitious and the closest to the Vir Saini reel that earned 23.4M. It is also the one that makes the argument for robotics in schools. |
| 4 | 2. Robot Bachao | Invites every viewer to comment on their own school. |
| 5 | 4. Papa: 0, Class 7: 1 | Simple to shoot, easy to tag a parent in. |

Then 5, 3, 9, 7, 8.

Posting: one a week. Put the admissions line and phone number in the caption, not the video. Boost whatever passes about 25,000 views in 72 hours (three times the account's current median of 8,500).

## 6. Things to settle before shooting

1. **Consent.** Written parental consent for every child, and agreement from every teacher and parent who acts.
2. **Nobody is the butt of the joke except adults and the robot.** No child is mocked, scared or tricked on camera. The prank formats in the sample were left out for this reason.
3. **Which robots the school has.** Ideas 5, 9 and 10 need a remote-driven or programmable robot that moves reliably; idea 8 needs one that stops at an obstacle.
4. **The principal and the NDA instructor** for ideas 5 and 9.
5. **Music.** Original or licensed tracks only, so the same cut can run as a boosted post.
6. **No real brands, films or named schools** in any parody.
